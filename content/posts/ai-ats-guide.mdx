## Why Start with AI + ATS?

The keyword research brief highlighted that long-tail phrases such as **"how to use AI to build a resume"** and **"how to make an ATS friendly resume with AI"** sit in the KD 0-20 range. That means you can target highly specific user needs with less competition **and** match the same search terms inside your resume copy. When you speak the language of the job post, both hiring managers and automated tracking systems notice.

## The 5-Part Workflow

1. **Choose the right tool.** Pick an AI resume builder that lets you edit structure, run instant ATS checks, and export to DOCX/PDF. The brief specifically calls out features like template libraries, built-in ATS validators, and instant downloads.
2. **Import the job description.** Feed the tool 2-3 job ads. This helps the AI highlight the competencies, action verbs, and keyword density ranges (1-2%) that the strategy deck recommends.
3. **Draft with structured prompts.** Use instructions such as `"Write a professional summary for a remote lifecycle marketing manager that mentions retention, cohort analysis, and Braze."` Layer in the question-based keywords from Tier 2 to cover reader objections.
4. **Run ATS optimization.** Cross-check the AI draft against the ATS checklist in the research doc: consistent headings (H2/H3), bullet formatting, keyword reuse, and optional schema-ready sections like "Core Skills" or "Tools."
5. **Add proof and CTA.** The conversion tips section reminds us to add before/after metrics, testimonials, or CTA-style statements ("Ready to discuss a lifecycle audit?"). These human signals complement the AI polish.
